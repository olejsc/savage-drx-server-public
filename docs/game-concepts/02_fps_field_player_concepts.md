# Savage XR / Battle for Newerth - FPS / Field Player Concepts

## Field player role

Field players are the active pieces on the battlefield. They fight, mine, build, repair, scout, escort, defend, and assault. A commander can direct the team, but field players still decide how to move, aim, engage, retreat, or improvise.

A good field player understands that personal combat skill is only part of the job. The best players also know when to:

- mine Red Stone to accelerate tech;
- defend workers and unfinished structures;
- escort siege units;
- repair damaged buildings;
- attack key enemy tech structures;
- capture or defend spawn flags;
- follow commander or officer orders;
- switch roles when the team needs support.

## Core field priorities

| Situation | Good field-player response |
|---|---|
| Early game | Protect workers, mine if asked, scout enemy routes, harass enemy workers, and avoid wasting gold. |
| Your base is under attack | Return if the threat is real, identify the threat, kill or repel it, then repair. |
| Enemy is expanding | Attack unfinished structures quickly; unfinished buildings represent wasted enemy resources. |
| Commander places a forward base | Build first, fight second unless enemies are already on top of it. |
| Siege is moving | Escort and screen for flankers; healers stay behind cover. |
| Key enemy defense exists | Focus Shield Tower/Healing Spire before randomly damaging other structures. |

## Ranged combat principles

Ranged combat often shifts the camera to first-person and reduces awareness. Players should deliberately check flanks, behind, and above.

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| Reduced vision | Switch out of ranged view or look around often. Avoid tunnel vision. |
| Cover vs concealment | Cover blocks projectiles; concealment only hides you. Escape behind real cover. |
| Know range | Firing outside weapon range wastes ammo and reveals position. |
| Suppressive fire | Repeater-style or group fire can force enemy units to hide and create room for allies. |
| Lead targets | Slow projectiles and ping require aiming ahead of moving targets. |
| Never stop moving | Stationary players are easy targets, especially for snipers and accurate weapons. |
| Mix melee and ranged | Step just outside melee range, fire a rapid/continuous weapon, then switch back when the enemy closes. |

## Close combat principles

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| Strafing | Step to the side to dodge attacks and line up counter-hits. |
| Timing | Learn enemy attack chains and punish recovery windows. |
| Tight defense | Avoid overcommitting to the final attack in a chain if it leaves you exposed. |
| Break patterns | Repeated timing or direction becomes predictable. Mix attacks, pauses, and movement. |
| Close the gap | Save stamina for a burst into melee range, especially against ranged users. |
| Play possum | Pretend not to notice an enemy, then turn and punish their approach. |
| Fake targets | Appear to attack one unit while actually targeting another. |
| Jump attacks | Jumping or using slopes can shift your hit angle and throw off enemy aim. |
| Estimate health | Track hits landed so you know when a blocked or glancing hit is enough to finish a target. |

## Human-specific combat concepts

Humans rely heavily on **blocking**. A successful block:

- lasts about 1 second;
- covers roughly a 180-degree forward arc;
- releases when struck or when the timing expires;
- reduces incoming damage by about 75%;
- can stun or expose an attacker in close combat.

### Human tactics

| Tactic | Summary |
|---|---|
| Blocking a series | Repeatedly reapply block with precise timing. Mistiming lets attacks through. |
| Block and circle | After a successful block, sidestep to the opponent's open side and punish. |
| Block ranged attacks | Predict rate of fire and block before impact; not all area or siege damage is blockable. |
| Block spamming | Repeated block taps reduce vulnerability but slow movement and make you an easier target. |
| Rapid Nomad attacks | Interrupt the exposed end of the chain with a quick block tap, then resume attacking/building. |
| Watch your back | Beasts can leap, cloak, or attack from above; use sound and frequent checks. |
| Relocater discipline | Place pads safely; use them to survive, defend, or fake retreats. |

### Human field identity

Humans are strong when they combine blocking, ranged pressure, sensors, relocators, and coordinated demolition or siege. They are weaker if players stand still, ignore flankers, or waste expensive units.

## Beast-specific combat concepts

Beasts revolve around **Leap**, stamina management, and attacking from angles that are hard for Humans to block.

### Beast tactics

| Tactic | Summary |
|---|---|
| Leaping attack | Leap into the enemy's open side; add bounce by tapping jump after leap. |
| Leaping mid-attack | Start an attack while stationary, then leap mid-swing to surprise a Human who lowers guard. |
| Stealth/sneak attacks | Use terrain, high ground, and Mist Shroud to strike unaware players. |
| Staying out of range | Strafe outside melee range until the Human commits, then leap in. |
| Wrapping attacks | Leap past a Human and swing around their defense, forcing a 180-degree reaction. |
| Frontal swipe | Strike the edge of the Human block with momentum, aiming to hit or push out of counter range. |
| Rabid/Carnivorous sustain | Use successful melee hits to restore stamina or health and continue pressure. |

### Beast stamina rule

Keep enough stamina for at least one leap or escape. The guide notes that stamina recovers faster if kept above roughly one-quarter rather than fully drained.

## General combat statistics and regeneration

| Unit type | Health regeneration | HP after 1 minute |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | 2 HP every 5 seconds | 24 |
| Officer | 6 HP every 5 seconds | 72 |
| Beast using Rabid | 16 HP every 5 seconds | 192 |
| Non-officer siege | No regeneration bonus | NA |

## Attacking structures as a field player

### Human anti-structure tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Demolition Pack | 6000 building damage, large blast, but must survive an 8-second timer. Protect it by standing over it and blocking. |
| Mortar | High building damage, short range, bouncing/awkward arc. Best from level or high ground. |
| Launcher | Strong building damage and better range than mortar; low ammo. |
| Ballista | Long-range accurate siege. Test shots and listen for gold gain to confirm hits. |
| Catapult | Huge damage and HP but hard to aim and poor self-defense. |
| Electricity buff | 3x damage for 10 seconds; excellent on medium/heavy units attacking structures. |

### Beast anti-structure tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Sacrifice | 4500 damage in 80-unit radius after 10 seconds. Start before arrival so detonation happens at the target. |
| Blaze | Close-range anti-building and anti-siege pressure. |
| Fireball | Long-range building harassment and tower pressure. |
| Summoner | Long-range siege with unlimited shots but low HP. Use cover. |
| Behemoth | Close-range structure breaker; needs diversion or protection to arrive. |
| Fire Shield buff | Adds contact damage and helps Sacrifice/structure pushes. |

## Team tactics

| Operation | Required | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| TTD - Tower Take Down | 2 non-siege units, one with ranged weapon | Ranged player keeps tower/spire attention while melee player attacks. |
| TT - Tag Team | 2nd-tier siege officer + healer | Siege fires from cover while healer supports from full cover. |
| Human SPMin | Non-siege escort + siege + Chaplain | Escort screens attackers; Chaplain heals; siege keeps moving. |
| Human SPMed | Non-siege escort + Catapult + Ballista | Ballista protects Catapult and adds structure damage. |
| Human SPMaj | Two escorts + Catapult + Ballista + Chaplain | Stronger siege column with central healer/officer regeneration. |
| Human STD | Two non-siege units + two Electric buffs + two Demo Packs | Drop Demo Packs on opposite sides, switch positions to protect them, attack under Electric buff. |
| Human Diversion | Multiple infantry and siege units | Infantry wave draws defenders while siege gets into firing position. |
| Beast SPMed | Two escorts + two Behemoths + two Shamans | Escorts clear threats; Shamans Shield Ray Behemoths; Behemoths hit structures. |
| Beast SPMaj | Four escorts + two Behemoths + two Shamans | Adds wingmen and ranged support, often Tempest/Rupture. |
| Beast BS - Behemoth Shield | Behemoths lead, others follow | Behemoths absorb projectiles so unhurt attackers arrive behind them. |
| Beast STD | Two non-siege units + Fire Shield + Sacrifice | Fire-shielded attackers damage building, then Sacrifice. |
| Beast CS - Chain Sac | 3+ sacrificing units | Grouped, timed Sacrifice run; avoid distractions and target structures. |

## Siege player concepts

Siege units are high-value team assets. Do not grab siege randomly if the team cannot afford waste.

- Use cover and high ground.
- Move with escorts.
- Communicate before leaving base.
- Eject from Ballista if death is imminent so you can continue fighting as a Nomad.
- Summoners should expose themselves only briefly and use cover between shots.
- Behemoths should avoid open fields unless a diversion or healing/shield support is present.

## Healer concepts

Healers are low-HP support units but can save huge team resources by keeping expensive units alive or reviving them.

| Task | Chaplain | Shaman |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobility | Sprint | Leap + sprint |
| Building repair with melee | 215 HP/sec | 50 HP/sec |
| Healing units | 67 HP/sec | 80 HP per burst |
| Healing siege | 400 HP over 6 sec | 480 HP over 6 sec |
| Healing buildings | 400 HP over 6 sec | 480 HP over 6 sec |
| Recharge to full energy/mana | 13 sec | 13 sec |
| Extra support | Potions: 60 HP over 11 sec, starts with 12 | Shield Ray: 50% damage reduction over 50 sec |
| Defense | Blocking | Leap |
| Self-heal | Yes, with Potions | None |

### Chaplain abilities

| Ability | Requirement | Cost / research | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing Beam | Monastery | Free / none | Continuous short-range heal; full energy can restore 400 HP over 6 seconds. |
| Potions | Monastery | Free to player / 300 Red Stone / 25 sec | 60 HP over 11 seconds; can affect grouped units and siege. |
| Resurrect | Monastery | Free to player / 400 Red Stone / 35 sec | Revives teammates; grants 500 gold and 5 XP; user is invulnerable during cast. |

### Shaman abilities

| Ability | Requirement | Cost / research | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing Bolt | Sanctuary | Free / none | 80 HP per burst, about 1 burst per second; short range and aim-dependent. |
| Revive | Sanctuary | Free to player / 300 Red Stone / 25 sec | Revives fallen Beast teammates; does not work on siege. |
| Shield Ray | Sanctuary | Free to player / 400 Red Stone / 35 sec | 50% damage reduction on unit, siege, or building; lasts up to 50 seconds from full energy; does not stack. |

## Field communication

Use team chat for concise information, not noise. Examples:

- "1 enemy in base" is better than spamming "Return to base" when a single enemy is present.
- Call out enemy gateways, sensors, forward garrisons/sub-lairs, and siege positions.
- Officers should use `F` orders to mark attack targets, repair targets, defend targets, or move locations.

## Field-player summary

A strong field player is not only a duelist. Strong field play means combining mechanics, awareness, map movement, structure focus, economic discipline, and cooperation with commander/officer intent.
