# Savage XR / Battle for Newerth - General Game Concept

## High-level identity

*Savage: The Battle for Newerth* is an online multiplayer hybrid that combines a real-time strategy command layer with first-person/third-person action combat. One player on each team can command from an overhead RTS view while the rest of the team fights as autonomous field players. The commander places buildings, researches technology, directs workers, manages team resources, and issues orders; field players execute, improvise, fight, mine, scout, defend, and attack.

The guide frames the game as something close to "chess where all pieces have their own skills, weaknesses, and personalities." That is the core concept: strategy matters, but the pieces are human players, not obedient RTS units.

## Primary match premise

Two factions fight to destroy the enemy main base:

- **Legion of Man / Humans:** easier to learn, stronger overall ranged options, blocking-based melee defense, mechanical/industrial technology, portable items such as health packs, sensors, mines, demolition packs, disruptors, and relocators.
- **Beast Horde / Beasts:** stronger close combat and mobility, leap-based melee, nature/magic technology, relics such as sacrifice, frenzy, storm shield, mist shroud, and gateway-based mobility.

The classic mode is **Humans versus Beasts**, though the guide also mentions Humans vs Humans and Beasts vs Beasts.

## Victory and strategic pressure

The key structures are:

- **Human Stronghold**
- **Beast Lair**

When a team loses its main base, it loses the match. Everything else - forward bases, towers, resource mines, siege, healing support, and tech - exists to either protect your main base or create enough pressure to destroy the enemy's.

## Two simultaneous layers of play

### Field/action layer

Field players directly control units in action combat. They:

- fight in melee and ranged combat;
- build, repair, and mine;
- kill NPCs for gold, experience, ammo, or mana;
- escort siege and workers;
- capture spawn flags;
- harass workers and unfinished structures;
- defend against base incursions;
- coordinate attacks on key structures.

### Commander/RTS layer

The commander:

- places structures and towers/spires;
- controls up to 10 workers;
- researches weapons, units, equipment, relics, and upgrades;
- chooses technology routes;
- manages Red Stone and Gold;
- applies short-duration buffs;
- draws on the minimap and issues waypoints/orders;
- decides when to expand, defend, rush, or tech.

The commander has influence but not total control. Human players may follow orders, ignore them, or act independently.

## Core resources

| Resource | Role |
|---|---|
| Red Stone / Stone | Main strategic resource. Used for structures, upgrades, tech, and many commander actions. It is limited and map-controlled. |
| Gold | Player and team economy. Used for units, equipment, towers/spires, forward bases, and team support. Gold is earned through combat, NPCs, mining, structure damage, and other actions. |
| Commander tax | The guide notes a common tax where 46% of player-collected gold goes to the commander, adjustable on many servers. |

Red Stone usually determines tech speed and expansion potential. Gold determines how well players can equip themselves and how many expensive units or siege tools the team can field.

## Natural match phases

| Phase | Typical priorities |
|---|---|
| Early game | Build first tech structure, protect workers, mine Red Stone, prevent worker harassment, choose tech route, secure nearby spawn flags/mines, and launch early harassment. |
| Mid game | Establish forward bases, unlock medium units and weapons, start siege or anti-structure pressure, build key defensive towers/spires, and control more map resources than the opponent. |
| Late game | Use advanced units, siege, commander buffs, gateways/relocators, and coordinated structure takedowns to finish the enemy base. Protect your own key defense structures. |

## Faction contrast

| Topic | Humans | Beasts |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner learning curve | Easier to learn | Harder but very powerful when mastered |
| Ranged combat | Stronger, more accurate, more conventional weapons | Slightly shorter/less reliable on average, but Tempest and Lightning are dangerous |
| Melee defense/offense | Blocking, timing, retaliation after successful block | Leap mobility, attack wrapping, high-pressure melee |
| Signature anti-structure tools | Demolition Pack, Mortar, Launcher, Ballista, Catapult, Electricity buff | Sacrifice, Fireball, Blaze, Summoner, Behemoth, Fire Shield |
| Detection/mobility | Sensors and Relocaters | Mist Shroud, Sixth Sense, Gateway, Leap |
| Defensive core | Shield Tower, upgraded towers, sensors | Healing Spire, Flame/Wind Spires, Gateway counter-pressure |

## Progression

Players gain experience from killing enemies, killing NPCs, damaging or destroying buildings, building, repairing, mining, and surviving. Level bonuses include better laboring, armor, melee strength, stamina, Human shield, Beast improved leap, and higher hit points. ## Beginner principles

1. **Do not ignore base defense.** The main base is the king. Losing it ends the game.
2. **Do not only defend.** A team that never pressures the enemy usually loses map control and tech tempo.
3. **Protect workers.** Worker losses slow the commander dramatically.
4. **Use forward bases carefully.** A good Garrison/Sub-lair near a mine, choke, hill, or enemy base can decide the match.
5. **Know your role.** Some players should fight, some should mine/build, some should escort siege, and some should defend.
6. **Communicate clearly.** Text chat, voice commands, commander pings, and officer orders are critical.
7. **Attack key structures first.** Shield Towers, Healing Spires, Siege Workshops, Charm Shrines, and tech structures often matter more than random buildings.

## Overall design takeaway

The game is not only about personal kills. A strong team combines:

- mechanical combat skill;
- resource control;
- worker protection;
- tech timing;
- forward spawn placement;
- communication;
- siege coordination;
- timely commander buffs;
- disciplined attacks on key structures.
